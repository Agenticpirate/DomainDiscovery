## What Is the .dev Domain?

### Google Registry's Developer-Focused Extension

Like .app, the .dev extension is a gTLD operated by Google Registry. Google secured the rights to .dev during ICANN's new gTLD expansion program. The extension launched for general registration in February 2019 following a Founder's Access Program that allowed early registrants to secure names at premium prices.

The positioning from the start was clear: .dev is for the developer community. Not just people who build apps, but the broader ecosystem of coders, engineers, technical writers, open-source maintainers, and software craftspeople who form the backbone of the internet's infrastructure.

### The HTTPS Requirement

Just like .app, all .dev domains are included in the HSTS preload list. Every .dev domain must be served over HTTPS — HTTP connections are automatically blocked by modern browsers. This requirement is not a bug; it is a feature that aligns perfectly with developer values around security and best practices.

If you are comfortable with web development, setting up HTTPS is trivial. If you are just getting started, use a hosting platform that handles SSL automatically (Netlify, Vercel, GitHub Pages with a custom domain, Cloudflare Pages).

## .dev Domain Cost: Impressively Affordable

### Registration and Renewal Pricing

The .dev domain is one of the best value-for-money specialty extensions available. In 2026, typical prices range from **$12 to $18 per year** for registration and renewal. Some registrars offer first-year promotional rates as low as $9.

Compared to .io ($35–$60/year) and .ai ($70–$130/year), .dev is dramatically more affordable — often less than double the cost of a .com. For developers building personal projects, portfolios, or bootstrapped products, this makes .dev extremely accessible.

### Secondary Market Pricing

Clean, short .dev names — particularly common programming terms, framework names, or developer-focused keywords — trade at premiums on domain marketplaces. Expect to pay $500 to $5,000+ for established short .dev names on the secondary market.

## Why Developers Love .dev: The Community Angle

### Authenticity and Signal

Among developers, .dev carries an authentic signal that extensions like .xyz or generic gTLDs simply cannot replicate. When you see a .dev domain, you immediately understand the context: this is a developer's project, a developer's portfolio, or a tool built for developers.

This authenticity is hard to quantify but deeply real. The developer community has organically adopted .dev as its own, the same way it adopted GitHub as its social network or Stack Overflow as its knowledge base. Extensions do not typically earn this kind of community ownership — .dev has.

### Perfect for Personal Portfolios

The .dev domain has become the standard for developer portfolios. A pattern like firstname.dev or handle.dev is clean, professional, and immediately communicates technical competence to recruiters, hiring managers, and collaborators. Thousands of developers maintain personal sites at .dev addresses that serve as living resumes.

### Open-Source Project Homes

Open-source projects frequently use .dev domains as their official documentation or landing page homes. The extension signals to potential contributors that the project takes itself seriously without the formality of a .org domain.

## .dev Domain Pros and Cons

### Advantages

**Strong developer community resonance.** Within the technical community, .dev has genuine brand equity that has been earned organically over several years of adoption.

**Affordable pricing.** At $12–$18/year, .dev is one of the cheapest specialty extensions with genuine brand value.

**Google Registry reliability.** Same benefits as .app — professional registry management, strong infrastructure, low risk of instability.

**Mandatory HTTPS.** Enforces security best practices at the infrastructure level, which aligns with what serious developers want anyway.

**Excellent for personal branding.** Developers building a personal brand around a handle or name will find .dev natural, memorable, and respected.

**Clean visual aesthetic.** Short, lowercase, modern — .dev looks great on business cards, GitHub profiles, and conference slide decks.

### Disadvantages

**Limited to developer/technical audiences.** If your product has a mainstream or non-technical audience, .dev may create confusion about your product category.

**HTTPS is mandatory.** A minor friction point for beginners who have not dealt with SSL certificates before.

**Not ideal for general SaaS.** If your software product serves non-technical users (HR teams, accountants, marketers), .dev sends the wrong signal about your target audience.

**Some valuable names are taken.** The most obvious developer-relevant names — common programming terms, language names, framework names — were claimed quickly in 2019.

## .dev vs. .io: A Detailed Comparison

This is the comparison question that comes up most often in .dev domain review discussions. Both extensions are widely used in the tech community, but they serve different purposes and carry different costs.

### Cost

.dev wins decisively on cost at $12–$18/year versus .io at $35–$60/year. For bootstrapped projects or personal use, this gap is significant.

### Community Associations

.io has broader recognition across all of tech — SaaS, hardware, AI, developer tools, and more. .dev has more specific resonance within the coding and software development community itself.

### Target Audience Clarity

If you are building exclusively for developers, .dev sends a more precise signal. If your product serves a broader tech audience that includes non-engineers, .io's broader associations are an advantage.

### HTTPS Requirement

Both extensions require HTTPS through the HSTS preload list. This is not a differentiator — both mandate it.

### Availability

.dev generally has better name availability than .io for developer-relevant terms because .io had a five-year head start in adoption.

### The Verdict

For a developer portfolio or individual developer brand: .dev. For a SaaS product targeting a broad tech audience: .io or .dev are both viable — .io if budget allows, .dev if cost efficiency matters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a .dev domain used for?

A .dev domain is used primarily for developer portfolios, open-source project documentation, developer tool company websites, technical blogs, API documentation, and coding education platforms. The extension signals to visitors that the site is created by or for software developers and engineers. It has also become the standard format for professional developer portfolio URLs, with thousands of developers using the firstnamelastname.dev or handle.dev pattern.

### How much does a .dev domain cost per year?

A .dev domain typically costs $12 to $18 per year for registration and renewal in 2026. Some registrars offer first-year promotional rates as low as $9. This makes .dev one of the most affordable specialty extensions with genuine brand value — significantly cheaper than .io ($35–$60/year) and .ai ($70–$130/year). Secondary market prices for premium .dev names range from $500 to $5,000+.

### Do .dev domains require HTTPS?

Yes, all .dev domains require HTTPS. Every .dev domain is included in the HSTS (HTTP Strict Transport Security) preload list, which means modern browsers automatically block HTTP connections on .dev sites and require encrypted HTTPS. This mandatory security requirement is a deliberate design feature of the .dev extension. Modern hosting platforms like Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, and GitHub Pages provision SSL certificates automatically.

### Who operates the .dev domain registry?

The .dev domain registry is operated by Google Registry, a division of Alphabet Inc. Google secured the right to operate .dev through ICANN's new gTLD expansion program and launched it for general registration in February 2019. Google Registry also operates .app and .page. Google's own developer resources, including web.dev and flutter.dev, are hosted on .dev domains.

### Is .dev or .io better for a developer tool startup?

For a developer tool startup specifically targeting engineers and technical teams, .dev typically provides a stronger, more precise signal at lower cost. For a broader SaaS product that serves both technical and non-technical users, .io's broader tech associations may be more appropriate. Cost-wise, .dev at $12–$18/year is dramatically cheaper than .io at $35–$60/year, making it the better choice for bootstrapped founders or cost-conscious startups.

### Can personal developers use a .dev domain for a portfolio?

Absolutely — personal developer portfolios are one of .dev's primary use cases. The firstnamelastname.dev or handle.dev format has become a community standard for professional developer identity online. A .dev portfolio URL signals technical competence to recruiters and hiring managers immediately. Many developers use their .dev domain as a central hub linking to their GitHub, LinkedIn, published projects, and technical writing.

### Does using a .dev domain affect Google search rankings?

No. Google treats .dev as a standard gTLD with global indexing and no geographic restrictions. Rankings for .dev sites are determined by the same factors as any other domain: content quality, backlinks, site speed, mobile performance, and technical SEO. The mandatory HTTPS provides a minor positive ranking signal since Google uses HTTPS as a lightweight ranking factor. There is no negative SEO consequence for using .dev.
