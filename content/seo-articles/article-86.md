# How to Transfer a Domain Name to Another Registrar (Step-by-Step)

> **Quick Answer:** To transfer a domain name, unlock it at your current registrar, request your EPP/authorization code, then initiate the transfer at the new registrar by entering the domain name and EPP code. Approve the confirmation email, and the transfer completes within 1–7 days. Transfers add one year to your registration and cannot occur within 60 days of the domain's original registration.

Maybe you found a better price. Maybe your current registrar's interface drives you crazy. Maybe you're consolidating a portfolio of domains into one account for easier management. Whatever your reason, transferring a domain name to another registrar is a common task — and it's straightforward once you understand the process.

This guide walks you through exactly how to transfer a domain name, step by step, including what can go wrong and how to avoid those pitfalls.

**Key Facts for 2026:**
- Domain transfers are free to initiate from any ICANN-accredited registrar — the only cost is the mandatory 1-year renewal fee at the receiving registrar
- The EPP authorization code (also called an auth code or transfer key) expires within 24 hours at some registrars — always use it immediately after requesting it
- ICANN's 60-day transfer lock rule applies after both new registrations and completed transfers — plan portfolio consolidations accordingly
- Most domain transfers complete in 24–72 hours when the approval email is confirmed manually; the 5-day maximum is a fallback if you don't respond

## Why People Transfer Domain Names

Before diving into the mechanics, it's worth understanding the most common reasons people go through the domain transfer process:

- **Cost savings**: Registrar renewal prices vary significantly. Moving to a cheaper registrar (like Namecheap) can save $5–$10 per domain per year
- **Better interface or tools**: Some registrars have better dashboards, DNS management, or customer support
- **Account consolidation**: Managing 20 domains across 5 registrars is messy — transferring to one registrar simplifies things
- **Selling a domain**: Domain sales sometimes require transferring to the buyer's preferred registrar
- **Privacy and security**: Some registrars offer better WHOIS privacy or two-factor authentication options

## Understanding How Domain Transfers Work

A domain transfer moves the registration record of a domain from one ICANN-accredited registrar to another. The process is standardized across all major registrars and governed by ICANN rules.

Key facts about the domain transfer process:

- Transfers add 1 year to your registration (you pay for a full year at the receiving registrar)
- Most transfers complete within 5–7 days, though many finish in 24–48 hours
- You cannot transfer a domain that was registered or previously transferred within the last 60 days
- The domain's expiration date extends by one year from the current expiration (not from the transfer date)
- You need an EPP/authorization code (also called an auth code or transfer key) from your current registrar

## Step-by-Step: How to Transfer Your Domain Name

### Step 1: Choose Your Destination Registrar

Before initiating the transfer, decide where you want to move your domain. Popular destination registrars include:

- **Namecheap**: Competitively priced, excellent privacy features, clean interface
- **Cloudflare Registrar**: Charges at-cost pricing (no markup), great for DNS management
- **Porkbun**: Low prices, free WHOIS privacy included
- **Google Domains (now Squarespace Domains)**: Clean and simple, good integration with Google services
- **GoDaddy**: Largest registrar, massive support resources

Compare transfer pricing (which includes a 1-year renewal) before choosing. The cheapest renewal isn't always the cheapest first-year transfer.

### Step 2: Unlock Your Domain at the Current Registrar

Domain names have a "transfer lock" (also called Registrar Lock) that prevents unauthorized transfers. You need to disable this before initiating a transfer.

**How to unlock your domain**:
1. Log into your current registrar's account
2. Navigate to your domain management settings
3. Find the "Domain Lock" or "Transfer Lock" option
4. Toggle it to "Unlocked" or "Off"

This change usually takes effect immediately.

### Step 3: Get Your EPP/Authorization Code

The EPP code (also called an auth code, authorization code, or transfer key) is a unique password that proves you're authorized to transfer the domain. Without it, the receiving registrar cannot complete the transfer.

**How to get your EPP code**:
- Log into your current registrar
- Go to domain settings
- Look for "Auth Code," "EPP Code," or "Transfer Key"
- Request it (some registrars email it; others display it immediately)

Keep this code private. Anyone with your EPP code can transfer your domain without further authentication.

### Step 4: Verify Your Administrative Email Is Accessible

ICANN requires that domain transfer approvals be sent to the administrative contact email address on file. Make sure you have access to that email address before initiating the transfer.

If your admin email is an old address you no longer control, update your WHOIS contact information first and wait for the change to propagate before initiating the transfer.

**Note**: If your domain has WHOIS privacy enabled, the privacy email (which forwards to you) is typically still valid for transfer approval purposes.

### Step 5: Initiate the Transfer at the New Registrar

Go to your destination registrar (for example, Namecheap), navigate to their domain transfer section, and enter your domain name. You'll be prompted for:

- The domain name you want to transfer
- Your EPP/authorization code
- Payment (the transfer fee plus 1 year of renewal)

After paying, the new registrar submits a transfer request to your current registrar.

### Step 6: Approve the Transfer

Within a few hours, you'll typically receive an email from either your current registrar or ICANN asking you to approve the transfer. Click the approval link.

If you don't approve it manually, the transfer will auto-complete after 5 days (per ICANN rules) — but approving it manually speeds up the process significantly.

### Step 7: Wait for the Transfer to Complete

After approval, the transfer typically completes within 24–72 hours. Your domain's nameservers and DNS settings remain unchanged during the transfer unless you manually update them.

Once complete, you'll receive a confirmation email from the new registrar, and the domain will appear in your new account.

## Common Domain Transfer Problems (And How to Fix Them)

### "Domain is locked" Error

Your domain is still transfer-locked at the current registrar. Go back to Step 2 and disable the lock. Wait 15–30 minutes and try again.

### EPP Code Not Working

EPP codes expire after a certain period (varies by registrar, often 24 hours). If yours isn't working, request a new one.

### "Domain is within 60-day lock period" Error

ICANN requires domains to be held at a registrar for 60 days before they can be transferred. Wait until the 60-day period has elapsed after the original registration or last transfer.

### Didn't Receive the Approval Email

Check your spam folder. Also verify that the admin email address in your WHOIS record is correct and accessible. If it's wrong, update it first and allow propagation time.

### Transfer Fails After 5 Days

The most common cause is the current registrar declining the transfer. This can happen if:
- Your account has outstanding payment issues
- The domain's expiration is within 15 days (some registrars won't process transfer)
- You have an active dispute on the account

Contact your current registrar's support to resolve any holds.

## Important Rules to Know About Domain Transfers

**60-day lock after transfers**: Once a domain transfers, you cannot transfer it again for 60 days. Plan your consolidation strategy accordingly.

**Transfers add one year**: Even if your domain expires in 11 months, the transfer adds a full year to your registration from the current expiry date.

**Nameservers stay the same**: Unless you specifically change them, your DNS settings and nameservers remain identical through the transfer. Your website stays up throughout the process.

**WHOIS privacy may reset**: Some registrars require you to re-enable WHOIS privacy at the new registrar. Check your privacy settings after the transfer completes.

## Expert Verdict

Domain transfers are one of the most routine operations in domain management — and one of the most frequently mishandled. The most common source of confusion is the EPP code, which has different names (auth code, transfer key, authorization code) at different registrars and which expires faster than most users expect. Requesting the EPP code and immediately entering it at the receiving registrar eliminates this problem entirely.

The second most common issue is an inaccessible admin email address. ICANN requires transfer approval to be sent to the registered administrative contact. If you've changed email providers or the WHOIS privacy proxy address isn't forwarding correctly, the transfer stalls. Verifying administrative email access before initiating any transfer saves significant frustration.

For domain investors consolidating portfolios or sellers preparing to transfer domains to buyers, understanding ICANN's 60-day lock rule is essential planning knowledge. A domain that was just transferred cannot be moved again for 60 days, which can complicate time-sensitive sales if the buyer wants the domain at a different registrar. Planning transfers at least 60 days before anticipated sale negotiations is sound portfolio hygiene.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a domain transfer take?
Most domain transfers complete within 24–72 hours when the approval email is confirmed manually. If you don't confirm manually, the transfer auto-completes after 5 days per ICANN rules. Factors that extend the timeline include payment verification delays at the receiving registrar and weekend/holiday processing at some registrars.

### Does transferring a domain affect my website?
No. Your nameservers and DNS settings remain unchanged during a domain transfer. Your website, email, and all associated services continue to function normally throughout the process. Only if you manually change nameservers after the transfer would your site be affected.

### Why can't I transfer my domain? It says it's locked.
Domains have a "registrar lock" or "transfer lock" enabled by default to prevent unauthorized transfers. You must disable this lock in your current registrar's domain management settings before initiating a transfer. The unlock usually takes effect immediately, but wait 15–30 minutes before attempting the transfer.

### What is an EPP code and where do I find it?
An EPP code (also called an authorization code, auth code, or transfer key) is a unique password that authorizes a domain transfer. Find it in your current registrar's domain management panel — look for "Auth Code," "EPP Code," or "Transfer Authorization." Some registrars email it to you upon request; others display it directly. Use it within 24 hours as many registrars expire it quickly.

### Does transferring a domain extend its registration?
Yes. Every domain transfer includes a mandatory 1-year renewal at the receiving registrar. This year is added to the current expiration date — so if your domain expires in 8 months and you transfer it, it will now expire in 20 months. You pay for this renewal as part of the transfer fee.

### Can I transfer a domain I just registered?
No. ICANN's 60-day lock rule prevents transferring a domain that was registered within the last 60 days. The same 60-day lock applies after any completed transfer. Wait until the 60-day period has elapsed from the registration date before initiating a transfer.

### What happens to my DNS and email after a domain transfer?
Your DNS settings and nameservers carry over to the new registrar unchanged. Your website and email continue to work without interruption. However, some registrars reset WHOIS privacy after a transfer — check your privacy settings at the new registrar and re-enable privacy protection if needed.
