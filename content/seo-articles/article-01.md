### GoDaddy

## Domain Transfers: Moving from GoDaddy to Namecheap

If you are currently with GoDaddy and want to switch to Namecheap after reading this comparison, the process is straightforward:

### How to Transfer Your Domain

1. **Unlock your domain** in GoDaddy's dashboard under Domain Settings
2. **Disable domain privacy** temporarily (required for some transfer processes)
3. **Request an EPP/authorization code** from GoDaddy — this is delivered to your registrant email
4. **Initiate the transfer** at Namecheap using the auth code
5. **Confirm via email** — both registrars will send confirmation requests
6. **Wait 5–7 days** for the transfer to complete

A few important rules: domains must be at least 60 days old, and you cannot transfer a domain that was already transferred within the past 60 days. Most transfers also add one free year of registration to the domain at the new registrar's rate — so you effectively get a discounted year as part of the process.

GoDaddy's promotional pricing is a well-documented bait-and-switch strategy — the $0.99 or $1.99 first-year price masks a renewal rate of $21.99 that kicks in immediately afterward. When you add $9.99/year for WHOIS privacy (which Namecheap includes for free), GoDaddy's true annual cost of $31.98 per .com domain is more than double what Namecheap charges.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Namecheap actually cheaper than GoDaddy?
Yes. Namecheap is significantly cheaper than GoDaddy for most domain registrations and renewals in 2026. Namecheap charges $13.98/year to renew a .com domain with free WHOIS privacy included. GoDaddy charges $21.99/year for renewal plus $9.99 for privacy protection, bringing the true annual cost to approximately $31.98 — more than double Namecheap's total.

### Why is GoDaddy so expensive for renewals?
GoDaddy uses a promotional pricing strategy that offers very low first-year prices ($0.99 to $1.99 for .com) to attract customers, then charges substantially higher renewal rates to generate profit. This model also relies on paid add-ons like WHOIS privacy that competing registrars include for free. The $21.99/year .com renewal rate is among the highest of any major registrar.

### Does GoDaddy include free WHOIS privacy?
No. GoDaddy charges approximately $9.99 per year per domain for WHOIS privacy protection. In contrast, Namecheap, Porkbun, Cloudflare, and most other reputable registrars in 2026 include WHOIS privacy for free with every eligible domain.

### Is it safe to transfer a domain from GoDaddy to Namecheap?
Yes, domain transfers between ICANN-accredited registrars are a standard, well-supported process. Transferring from GoDaddy to Namecheap typically takes 5–7 days and usually includes a one-year extension on your domain registration at the new registrar's rate. Domains must be at least 60 days old and not transferred within the last 60 days to be eligible.

### What is better than both Namecheap and GoDaddy?
For pure price, Cloudflare Registrar (~$9.15/year for .com) and Porkbun (~$10.98/year for .com) are cheaper than Namecheap and far cheaper than GoDaddy. Porkbun also includes free SSL certificates and basic email hosting. However, Cloudflare has limited TLD selection and complex outbound transfers, making it more suitable for technical users already in the Cloudflare ecosystem.

### How much can I save by switching from GoDaddy to Namecheap?
On a single .com domain held for five years, switching from GoDaddy to Namecheap saves approximately $65. For a portfolio of 10 domains over five years, the savings exceed $600. The exact amount depends on the TLDs you own and whether you are currently paying for GoDaddy's privacy protection.

### Does Namecheap have good customer support?
Yes. Namecheap offers 24/7 live chat support, which is widely regarded as one of the best support channels in the domain registration industry. Response times are typically under five minutes. GoDaddy offers 24/7 phone and chat support with wider channel availability, but reviews frequently cite inconsistent quality and upsell-focused interactions.
