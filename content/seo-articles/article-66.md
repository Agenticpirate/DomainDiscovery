## Step 1: Define Your Naming Brief Before Prompting AI

The quality of AI output is entirely dependent on the quality of your input. Before you open any AI domain name generator, take five minutes to define:

### Your Brand Personality
Choose three to five adjectives that describe how you want your brand to feel. Examples:
- "Modern, minimal, premium, global"
- "Warm, accessible, community-focused, local"
- "Technical, precise, developer-friendly, fast"

### Your Target Audience
A B2B SaaS tool for enterprise IT teams should have a completely different name than a D2C wellness brand targeting millennial women. Define your primary buyer in one sentence.

### Your Industry and Competitive Context
List two or three competitors and describe how you want to differentiate. If all your competitors have clinical, serious names, a more human name may stand out. If they all have playful names, something more authoritative might differentiate you.

### Your Length and Format Preferences
- One word or two?
- Real word or invented?
- Should it hint at your product category, or be completely abstract?

## Step 2: Write Effective AI Prompts for Domain Names

Most people prompt AI domain generators by typing something like "names for my marketing agency." This produces mediocre results. Here are better approaches:

### Prompt Template 1: Structured Brief
> "Generate 20 domain name ideas for a [describe product/service]. The brand should feel [adjectives]. My target customer is [describe customer]. The name should be [one/two words], easy to spell and pronounce, and ideally available as a .com. Avoid anything that sounds like [competitor names]. Include both invented words and creative combinations."

### Prompt Template 2: Metaphor Anchoring
> "My [product type] helps [customer] achieve [outcome]. Generate 15 domain name ideas using metaphors from [nature/architecture/navigation/science]. Each should be under 12 characters and globally pronounceable."

### Prompt Template 3: Iterative Refinement
Start with a broad prompt, then ask AI to refine:
> "From these 20 names, identify the five that sound most [premium/playful/technical]. For each, generate five variations that maintain the same feel but are more distinctive."

## Step 3: Evaluate AI-Generated Domain Name Suggestions

Not all AI domain name suggestions are worth pursuing. Use this evaluation framework:

### The Phonetic Test
Say the name aloud five times quickly. Does it feel natural? Does it create any unintended sounds when spoken? Does it work across accents?

### The Spelling Test
If someone hears the name for the first time in a podcast, can they spell it correctly? Names that require creative spelling ("Flikr" style) add friction.

### The Google Test
Search the name on Google. Does it return results for a competitor, a negative concept, or a trademark? If it returns nothing, that is ideal.

### The .com Priority Test
Check whether the .com is available. If it is not, is the name distinctive enough to own on an alternative TLD without confusing customers?

### The 48-Hour Test
Write down your top three choices and do not look at them for 48 hours. Which one still excites you? That is usually the right choice.

## Common Mistakes When Using AI Domain Name Generators

### Accepting the First Round of Output

AI generates its best work when you iterate. Treat the first round of suggestions as a starting point, not a final list.

### Ignoring Brand Fit

A name can be creative and available but still wrong for your brand positioning. Always filter through your brand brief.

### Forgetting to Check Trademarks

AI cannot check trademark databases. A name being available as a domain does not mean it is legally available as a brand. Always verify through USPTO or EUIPO.

### Over-Relying on a Single Tool

Different AI tools have different strengths. Combine ChatGPT with domain-specific AI generators for the broadest range of creative options.

### How do I write a good prompt for an AI domain name generator?
Include four elements in your prompt: what your business does (one sentence), how you want the brand to feel (three to five adjectives), who your primary customer is (one sentence), and your format preferences (one word or two, real word or invented, character limit). Specific prompts produce specific, usable output. Vague prompts like "names for my startup" produce generic keyword combinations.

### Can I trust AI to check if a domain name is available?
No. AI language models do not have real-time access to domain registration databases. ChatGPT or Claude can generate candidate names, but they cannot tell you which are actually available to register today. Always cross-reference AI suggestions with a live domain availability tool. DomainsDiscovery.com checks availability across 1,600+ TLDs in real time and supports bulk searches for entire AI-generated name lists.

### How many names should AI generate before I check availability?
Generate at least 20–30 names before checking availability. The goal is to build a pool large enough that you have strong options remaining after availability and trademark filtering eliminate the inevitable losers. If you check availability after five names and all five are taken, you have to start over. With 30 names, you will almost always find at least three to five strong options still available on your preferred TLD.

### Should AI-generated names be real words or invented words?
Both work, but invented words have a significant availability advantage in 2026. Real words — especially common English words and obvious compounds — are overwhelmingly registered as .com domains. AI-generated invented words that follow natural phonetic patterns (ending in vowels, globally pronounceable, under 8 characters) are far more likely to be available at standard registration pricing and have the added advantage of being fully trademarkable from day one.

### How do I check if an AI-generated name is safe in other languages?
