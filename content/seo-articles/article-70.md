## Why Most People Spend Too Long on Business Naming

The naming paralysis most founders experience comes from a fundamental process error: they try to evaluate names at the same time they are generating them. These are two different cognitive tasks. When you mix them, you get stuck in a loop of generating, rejecting, second-guessing, and starting over.

The 30-minute process below separates generation from evaluation, which eliminates the loop.

## Minutes 0–5: Define Your Naming Criteria

Before generating a single name idea, answer these four questions in writing. Do not overthink them — write your first honest answers.

### Question 1: What does your business do in one sentence?

Not a pitch. Not a value proposition. A plain description.
"I make custom wooden furniture for small apartments."
"I run a SaaS product that automates employee onboarding."
"I am a freelance graphic designer specializing in brand identity."

### Question 2: How do you want customers to feel when they interact with your brand?

Choose three adjectives. Examples: "trusted, approachable, expert" / "bold, fast, innovative" / "warm, personal, reliable"

### Question 3: Who is your primary customer?

One sentence. "Small business owners in the US with 5–50 employees." "First-time homebuyers in urban markets." "B2B SaaS founders raising their first round."

### Question 4: What naming style fits your business?

- **Descriptive:** the name explains what you do (e.g., ClearBookkeeping.com)
- **Brandable/invented:** the name is distinctive but not immediately descriptive (e.g., Canva)
- **Personal name:** you as the brand (e.g., JaneDoeDesign.com)
- **Geographic:** local focus is a feature (e.g., ChicagoRoofingPros.com)

## Minutes 5–15: Generate 20 Name Candidates Without Filtering

Set a timer for 10 minutes. Your only goal is to produce 20 name candidates. Do not evaluate, do not check domains, do not reject anything. Write every idea down no matter how bad it seems.

### Naming Techniques to Use During Generation

**Technique 1: Core word brainstorm**
Write down 10 words directly related to what your business does or how it makes customers feel. For a productivity app: flow, clarity, system, pace, track, build, sprint, focus, clear, pulse.

**Technique 2: Thesaurus expansion**
Take your two strongest words and look them up in a thesaurus. Add five synonyms for each to your list. Look especially for uncommon variants you would not have thought of otherwise.

**Technique 3: Compound combinations**
Combine pairs of words from your list. "FlowPulse," "ClearSprint," "BuildPath," "FocusTrack." Write every combination — you will filter later.

**Technique 4: Invent a word**
Take one of your core words and modify it: change a vowel, add a suffix (-ly, -io, -ify, -er, -ify), drop a letter. "Focusly," "Clarily," "Pulsify," "Tracknest."

**Technique 5: Metaphor mining**
Think of a metaphor for what your business delivers. A financial advisor delivers "a map through complexity" — so name ideas from navigation: Waypoint, Compass, Meridian, Beacon, Landmark.

By the end of 10 minutes, you should have at least 20 candidates.

## Minutes 15–20: Apply the Three-Filter Test

Now evaluate your list. Move quickly — you have five minutes. Apply three filters in sequence.

### Filter 1: The Spelling Test (Eliminate)

Cross out any name that would require you to spell it out if you said it aloud over the phone. If you have to say "it is spelled C-L-A-R-I-L-Y with a Y at the end," that is a problem.

### Filter 2: The Five-Second Memory Test (Eliminate)

Read each remaining name once, then close your eyes. Which ones do you remember 30 seconds later? Cross out the ones that did not stick.

### Filter 3: The Brand Fit Test (Prioritize)

Of the names that survived filters 1 and 2, mark the three that best match the adjectives you chose in the criteria step. These are your top three candidates.

---

## Minutes 20–25: Check Domain Availability

With your top three candidates in hand, you now check domain availability. Do this as a bulk search so you can see all options simultaneously rather than searching each name one at a time.

### What to Search For

For each of your three candidates, search:
- [Name].com
- [Name].co
- [Name].io
- [Name].ai (if you are in a tech or AI-adjacent field)
- [Name].app
- [Name].net

A bulk domain search tool lets you enter all 15–18 combinations at once and see availability instantly.

### What to Do If Everything Is Taken

If all three top candidates are unavailable on every reasonable TLD, do not panic. Return to your 20-name list and pick three more. Alternatively, try these modification strategies:

- Add a geographic word: "[Name]HQ.com," "[Name]Lab.com"
- Add a function word: "[Name]App.com," "[Name]Studio.com," "Get[Name].com"
- Try a different TLD you had not considered
- Invent a variation of your top choice

### What to Do When You Find an Available Domain

Resist the urge to immediately register. Complete the full 30-minute process first — you may find something better in the next five minutes.

## Minutes 25–30: Make the Decision and Register

You have a shortlist of available options. Now decide.

### The Decision Framework

**If one name clearly excites you more than the others:** That is your answer. Register it.

**If you cannot choose between two names:** Ask one other person (just one — committees ruin naming decisions). Tell them your brand adjectives and ask which name fits better. Take their answer.

**If none of the available options feel right:** Give yourself one more 10-minute generation round with fresh constraints. Sometimes the second round produces better work because you have warmed up your thinking.

### Registering Your Business Name and Domain

Once you have decided:

1. Register your primary domain
2. Register the .com version if you are launching on a different TLD
3. Register your most likely misspelling
4. Claim social media handles on every relevant platform

Do all of this within 24 hours of deciding. Ideas get slow-leaked through conversations, drafts, and browser history — domain squatters have sophisticated monitoring systems.

## After the 30 Minutes: Protecting Your Business Name

The domain registration is just the first step. Within 90 days of launching, also:

- **File for trademark protection** in your operating jurisdiction
- **Set up professional email** through your domain (e.g., you@yourbusiness.com)
- **Redirect common variations** to your main domain
- **Enable domain auto-renewal** so you do not accidentally lose it

## A Note on Perfectionism in Business Naming

The best business name is the one your customers come to know through consistent, excellent work. Instagram was almost called "Burbn." Google was almost called "Backrub." The name mattered far less than the product, the marketing, and the decade of sustained execution.

Give yourself 30 minutes, follow the process, and move on. Your business is not built on its name — but it does need one to get started.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I really come up with a good business name in 30 minutes?
Yes, if you separate generation from evaluation. The naming paralysis most founders experience comes from evaluating names while generating them — a mixed cognitive task that creates endless loops. The 30-minute process prevents this by dedicating distinct time blocks to criteria definition, generation, evaluation, and availability checking. Thousands of successful businesses have been named in under an hour using structured processes like this one.

### What if all my name ideas are already registered as .com domains?
This is increasingly common with 165 million .com domains registered. When your top three candidates are all taken on .com, try three strategies: invent a word by modifying one of your candidates (change a vowel, add a suffix), search the same names on alternative TLDs (.co, .io, .app) at DomainsDiscovery.com, or return to your 20-name list for the next three options. In almost every case, expanding the search across all TLDs surfaces a viable option.

### Should I use AI to help with the 30-minute naming process?
Yes, AI can accelerate the generation phase significantly. Use it during Minutes 5–15 to expand your candidate list beyond what manual brainstorming produces. A 30-second AI prompt with your business description and brand adjectives can generate 20 additional candidates to supplement your manual list, dramatically increasing the pool before the evaluation filters run. The evaluation and availability steps remain human-driven.

### How do I check domain availability quickly for multiple names?
Use a bulk domain search tool that checks multiple names across multiple TLDs simultaneously. At DomainsDiscovery.com, you can enter your entire shortlist and see availability across 1,600+ extensions in a single search. This takes 60 seconds instead of 30 minutes of checking names one by one across different registrar sites. The time saving is significant enough to make the difference between registering your domain the same day you decide and losing it overnight.

### Is it okay to launch on a TLD other than .com?
Yes, with caveats. .co, .io, .ai, and .app are all credible alternatives for startups and tech businesses. The key requirements are that your audience is tech-savvy enough to recognize non-.com TLDs, your competitors do not all have .com (which would make you appear less established by comparison), and you have a clear plan to acquire the .com within three to five years as your brand grows. Consumer-facing businesses and those relying on audio advertising are better served by .com from launch.

### Should I get feedback on my name before registering?
One trusted person's opinion is valuable. More than one creates committee dynamics that produce the least-objectionable option rather than the best option. If you are torn between two candidates, ask one person who represents your target customer — not a friend or family member who may optimize for their own taste. Take their answer. Then register the same day, before additional opinions create new doubt.

### What should I do immediately after registering my domain?
Within 24 hours: claim matching social media handles on all relevant platforms, set up professional email through your domain, and enable domain auto-renewal at your registrar. Within 90 days: file for trademark protection in your operating jurisdiction, register common misspellings and redirect them to your main domain, and register your name on .com if you launched on another TLD. These steps transform a registered domain into a protected brand asset.
