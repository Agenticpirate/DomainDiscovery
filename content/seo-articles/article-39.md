## What Is the .tech Domain?

### Background and Registry

.tech is a generic top-level domain (gTLD) launched in 2015 as part of ICANN's new gTLD expansion program. It is operated by Radix Registry, the same company behind .store, .online, .site, and other commercial gTLDs. Radix positioned .tech specifically for technology companies, tech professionals, and tech-focused content creators.

Unlike ccTLDs such as .io (British Indian Ocean Territory) or .ai (Anguilla), .tech has no geographic associations. It is purely a descriptor — an extension that categorizes the type of business or content it hosts, not its location.

### Growth and Adoption

Since its launch, .tech has accumulated several million registered domains. Adoption spans individual tech professionals, startups, enterprise technology companies, tech events, academic institutions, and tech media brands. This breadth of adoption gives .tech a stronger claim to mainstream credibility than some other specialty extensions.

## .tech Domain Cost: Pricing in 2026

### Standard Registration and Renewal Fees

The .tech domain falls into a mid-range pricing tier. In 2026, typical registration costs range from **$30 to $50 per year**, with renewal rates in the same range. Some registrars offer first-year promotional pricing as low as $5–$10, with standard rates applying from year two onward.

This makes .tech more expensive than .online or .dev but comparable to .io for many use cases. For technology companies with professional branding needs, the moderate cost is generally acceptable.

### Premium .tech Domains

Radix designates certain high-value .tech names as premium domains with elevated registration costs. Terms like cloud.tech, security.tech, or ai.tech may carry premium prices ranging from hundreds to thousands of dollars. Check premium pricing during your domain search.

## Pros and Cons of the .tech Domain

### Advantages

**Explicit technology identity.** A .tech domain communicates your industry affiliation with zero ambiguity. Visitors encountering yourcompany.tech immediately understand they are dealing with a technology-oriented brand. This is particularly valuable in a sales or marketing context where first impressions shape perception.

**Better name availability than .com.** Common technology terms and brand names that have been registered in .com for years or decades are frequently available as .tech. Companies that have been squeezed out of their preferred .com can often find clean, descriptive .tech alternatives.

**No geographic restrictions.** Unlike ccTLDs used by tech companies (like .io or .ai), .tech has no country-of-origin associations. This can be advantageous for global brands that want a technology signal without geographic implications.

**Professional appearance.** In investor pitches, conference sponsorships, and B2B marketing materials, a .tech domain reads as intentional and professional. It does not have the spam reputation of .xyz or the unfamiliarity of more obscure extensions.

**Growing recognition.** As more notable technology brands adopt .tech, consumer and business familiarity with the extension continues to increase.

### Disadvantages

**Higher cost than some competitors.** At $30–$50/year, .tech is more expensive than .dev ($12–$18) and comparable to or more expensive than .io for some registrars.

**No specific niche community.** Unlike .dev (beloved by coders) or .ai (claimed by the AI industry), .tech is broad enough that it lacks the tight community identity of niche extensions. "Technology" covers so much ground that .tech does not signal specialization within tech.

**Less established than .io.** Despite strong growth, .tech has less historical brand equity than .io, which had a decade head start in tech startup adoption.

**Consumer familiarity below .com.** For consumer-facing technology products, mainstream audiences may be less familiar with .tech than with .com or established ccTLDs.

## .tech Domain SEO: What You Need to Know

### Google's Treatment of .tech

Google treats .tech as a standard gTLD. No geographic restrictions apply, and .tech domains are indexed and ranked using the same algorithmic factors as .com, .net, or any other gTLD. The extension is not a positive or negative ranking signal.

### Building SEO Authority on a .tech Domain

For technology companies building organic search presence on .tech domains, the effective strategy is:

**Establish topical authority.** Publish deep, expert content about your specific technology niche. A cybersecurity company on a .tech domain should publish authoritative content about cybersecurity topics. A cloud infrastructure provider should create comprehensive cloud computing resources.

**Earn quality backlinks.** Technology publications, industry blogs, partner websites, and academic institutions are valuable backlink sources for .tech domains. A mention in TechCrunch, Wired, or a respected developer blog carries significant authority.

**Technical excellence.** Technology audiences are particularly sensitive to site performance. Core Web Vitals scores, mobile optimization, and fast load times are non-negotiable for .tech sites targeting technical buyers.

**Structured data for tech products.** Implement schema markup for software applications, organization data, and articles to enhance search appearance.

## .tech vs. .io: Which Is Better for Technology Companies?

This is the most common comparison in any .tech domain review, and it deserves a detailed breakdown.

### Brand Recognition

.io has a significant head start — years of organic adoption by successful startups have built strong brand equity in the technology community. .tech is gaining, but the gap is real in 2026.

### Cost

This is where .tech can win depending on the registrar. Both extensions typically fall in the $30–$60/year range, but .tech promotional pricing is often more aggressive, creating opportunities for first-year savings.

### Specificity vs. Breadth

.io is associated with SaaS, developer tools, and general technology products. .tech is even broader — covering consumer electronics, enterprise IT, technology media, and more. If your audience specifically recognizes .io as a startup/developer signal, it may resonate more deeply.

### Geopolitical Risk

.io is a ccTLD with a small but non-zero geopolitical risk factor tied to the British Indian Ocean Territory's political status. .tech is a pure gTLD with no such risk.

### The Verdict

For established developer tools and SaaS companies: .io may still have stronger brand signal. For technology companies in enterprise IT, consumer electronics, technology media, or broader tech sectors: .tech is a compelling primary option. For companies prioritizing political stability and no geographic associations: .tech wins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the .tech domain used for?

The .tech domain is used by technology companies, IT service providers, tech media brands, consumer electronics companies, edtech platforms, coding bootcamps, technology event organizers, and technology associations. It communicates industry affiliation unambiguously — visitors encountering a .tech domain immediately understand they are interacting with a technology-oriented organization. Unlike more specific extensions like .dev or .ai, .tech is broad enough to work across all technology sectors.

### How much does a .tech domain cost per year?

A .tech domain typically costs $30 to $50 per year for standard registration and renewal in 2026. Some registrars offer first-year promotional pricing as low as $5–$10, with standard rates applying from year two. Premium .tech names — particularly category terms like cloud.tech, security.tech, or ai.tech — carry elevated registration prices set by Radix Registry.

### Is .tech better than .io for a technology company?

It depends on your specific use case. .io has stronger brand equity in the startup and developer tools community due to its longer adoption history. .tech is better suited for enterprise IT companies, consumer electronics brands, tech media, and technology organizations where .io's startup/developer connotation is a mismatch. .tech also eliminates the minor geopolitical risk associated with .io's British Indian Ocean Territory origin. Cost-wise, both extensions are in a similar annual range.

### Can a .tech domain rank on Google?

Yes. Google treats .tech as a standard gTLD with no geographic restrictions. .tech domains are indexed and ranked using the same algorithmic factors as .com: content quality, backlinks, Core Web Vitals, technical SEO, and site performance. There is no ranking penalty for .tech. Technology companies publishing authoritative content about their specific niche, earning quality backlinks, and maintaining technical excellence will rank effectively on .tech.

### What are the most well-known brands using .tech domains?

The most prominent .tech domain users include CES.tech (the Consumer Electronics Show's official site), TATA.tech (Tata Technologies, a subsidiary of India's TATA Group), and Salesforce.tech (Salesforce's developer-focused initiatives). Various national technology associations, coding education platforms, and tech event organizers also use .tech. These enterprise and industry-level adoptions validate .tech's fitness for professional use at significant scale.

### Is the .tech domain a good investment for a startup?

For a technology startup specifically, .tech is a reasonable domain investment if the company's product or service spans general technology without fitting neatly into the .ai, .dev, or .app categories. It reads as professional and intentional in investor materials and press coverage. The $30–$50 annual cost is modest for any funded startup. However, if the .com equivalent is available at a reasonable price, securing .com is generally advisable for maximum mainstream consumer recognition.
