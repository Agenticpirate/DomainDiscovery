## What Is the .store Domain Extension?

### A Purpose-Built eCommerce Extension

.store is a generic top-level domain (gTLD) launched in 2016 as part of ICANN's new gTLD expansion program. It is operated by Radix Registry, one of the largest registry operators in the new gTLD space. Radix specifically designed .store to serve the global eCommerce and retail market — and the extension has grown steadily, accumulating hundreds of thousands of registered domains.

Unlike ccTLDs or technically-oriented extensions like .io or .dev, .store carries an immediately commercial signal. It tells visitors, search engines, and business partners that the site in question is about buying and selling.

### Who Operates .store?

Radix Registry operates .store alongside a portfolio of other commercial and niche extensions including .online, .tech, .site, and others. Radix has invested in marketing and partnerships to drive adoption of .store among eCommerce brands and agencies.

## .store Domain Cost: Pricing in 2026

### Standard Registration Fees

The .store domain falls into a mid-range pricing tier. In 2026, typical registration costs range from **$20 to $35 per year**, with renewal rates in the same range. Introductory promotional pricing from certain registrars can drop first-year costs to $5–$10, but verify renewal rates carefully before committing.

Compared to .com ($10–$15/year), .store is moderately more expensive. Compared to .io ($35–$60/year) or .ai ($70–$130/year), it is reasonably priced for what it offers.

### Premium .store Domains

Radix designates certain high-value .store names as premium domains, with prices set higher at registration. Common retail terms like fashion.store, food.store, or electronics.store may be priced at $500–$5,000 or more for the initial registration. These names offer strong commercial potential for category-defining businesses.

## Pros and Cons of the .store Domain

### Advantages

**Crystal-clear commercial intent.** A .store domain communicates exactly what you are doing. Visitors who land on a .store site have zero confusion about the site's purpose — they are in a store. This clarity sets expectations and can pre-qualify visitors who intend to browse and buy.

**Descriptive branding opportunity.** .store enables clean, descriptive domain constructions: brand.store, productcategory.store, cityname.store. These can be more intuitive than forcing an eCommerce brand into an unrelated .com.

**Good name availability.** Compared to .com, which has been extensively registered for decades, .store offers significantly more availability of clean, commercial terms. Brands that cannot secure their preferred .com can often find the equivalent .store name available.

**Moderate cost.** At $20–$35/year, .store is accessible for small businesses and solo entrepreneurs without the premium cost of extensions like .ai or .io.

**Growing recognition.** As eCommerce has expanded globally, .store has built increasing recognition among online shoppers. The extension feels natural when shopping online.

### Disadvantages

**Less established trust than .com.** Despite growing recognition, .com remains the dominant signal of legitimacy for many online shoppers. Customers who have experienced fraud on unfamiliar extensions may be more cautious on a .store site.

**Conversion rate questions.** Some eCommerce practitioners report lower conversion rates on non-.com domains, attributed to user trust psychology. This is not universal and depends heavily on brand recognition, site design, and traffic source quality.

**Competitive disadvantage if .com is available.** If your preferred .com name is available and affordable, the argument for .store is weaker. .store shines most when your .com equivalent is taken or prohibitively expensive on the secondary market.

**SEO starting point.** New .store domains start with zero domain authority and must build it from scratch, the same as any new domain. There is no inherent SEO advantage to .store over .com.

## Real-World Brands Using .store Domains

### Notable eCommerce Brands

Several well-known brands have adopted .store domains:

- **Nike.store** — Nike uses a .store domain for certain regional or campaign-specific storefronts
- **Googlemerchandisestore.com** operates alongside **store.google.com** — Google uses "store" prominently in its retail branding
- **Apple** uses **apple.com/shop** but the .store extension aligns with how many tech brands are building dedicated commerce presences
- Various direct-to-consumer (DTC) brands have adopted brand.store as their primary commerce URL
- Independent Shopify and WooCommerce store owners frequently use .store for its descriptive clarity

The pattern among larger brands is often to use .store as a secondary or regional domain rather than replacing a .com entirely. Smaller brands and new entrants use it as a primary domain more frequently.

## .store Domain SEO: Can You Rank Effectively?

### Search Engine Treatment

Google treats .store as a standard gTLD. The extension itself is not a ranking factor — positive or negative. Your .store site will be indexed and ranked based on the standard set of signals: content quality, backlinks, Core Web Vitals, structured data, and on-page optimization.

### eCommerce SEO on a .store Domain

Running effective eCommerce SEO on a .store domain involves the same fundamentals as on any domain:

**Product page optimization.** Each product page should have unique, descriptive content with relevant keywords, optimized title tags, and proper schema markup for products (price, availability, review ratings).

**Category page authority.** Build category pages that serve informational intent as well as commercial intent. A category page for "organic skincare" should answer common questions about organic skincare, not just list products.

**Technical SEO fundamentals.** Fast load times, mobile responsiveness, clean URL structures, and proper canonical tags are non-negotiable for eCommerce sites regardless of domain extension.

**Backlink building.** Press mentions, influencer collaborations, and content marketing that earns editorial links will build domain authority over time on a .store domain just as effectively as on a .com.

## Who Should Use a .store Domain?

### Best Candidates

- **New eCommerce brands** that cannot secure their preferred .com name at a reasonable price
- **Product-specific stores** where a descriptive .store URL is more intuitive than a branded .com (e.g., organicsoaps.store vs. organicsoapsbyjane12345.com)
- **Shopify or WooCommerce merchants** looking for a clean professional URL to replace their default platform subdomain
- **Brands launching dedicated storefronts** separate from their main .com corporate site
- **Small to mid-size retailers** entering eCommerce for the first time with limited domain budget
- **Niche retailers** targeting specific categories where a descriptive .store name creates instant clarity

### Who Should Look Elsewhere

- **Businesses where .com is available** and reasonably priced — capturing the .com is almost always better for mainstream consumers
- **Service businesses** that sell services rather than physical or digital products — .store implies physical or digital goods
- **B2B companies** where the commercial connotations of .store may feel too consumer-facing
- **Content publishers** or media companies — .store sends the wrong category signal

## .store vs. .shop: What Is the Difference?

Both .store and .shop are eCommerce-focused extensions. The practical difference is subtle:

- **.store** has been available longer and has more brand recognition in the eCommerce space
- **.shop** is operated by GMO Registry and similarly targets retail brands
- Both are comparable in price and SEO treatment
- .store has a stronger presence among English-language Western brands; .shop has strong adoption in Asian markets

If you are undecided between the two, check availability for your preferred name on both extensions and choose based on which yields the cleaner, more memorable domain.

## Tips for Maximizing Your .store Domain

**Pair it with strong branding.** Your visual identity — logo, color palette, typography — does more to establish trust with new visitors than your domain extension. Invest in professional design.

**Use social proof prominently.** Customer reviews, trust badges, and social media follower counts visible above the fold can offset any extension-related trust hesitation from new visitors.

**Enable standard security signals.** HTTPS (SSL certificate), secure payment badges, and clear return policies all reinforce trust on a .store domain or any other extension.

**Build a content marketing strategy.** A blog or resource section on your .store domain that addresses buyer questions builds organic traffic and domain authority simultaneously.

The .store domain is a genuinely good choice for eCommerce businesses in 2026 — particularly for brands that cannot secure their preferred .com name at a reasonable price, or for merchants who want a domain name that immediately communicates commercial purpose. The extension is not a replacement for the universal trust of .com, but for the right business with the right execution, it is a perfectly legitimate and effective home for an online store.

## Expert Verdict

The .store domain has built a strong case as the most purpose-fit extension for eCommerce businesses in the new gTLD era. Its commercial signal is unambiguous — unlike .io or .tech, which require context to interpret, .store tells every visitor exactly what they will find before they even arrive. For categories where purchase intent is the primary visitor motivation, this pre-qualification effect is genuinely valuable.

Nike's adoption of Nike.store for regional storefronts is the extension's highest-profile credibility anchor. When a brand with Nike's resources and brand recognition chooses .store for its commerce presence, it validates the extension's fitness for purpose in a way that no registry marketing campaign could replicate. Combined with adoption by hundreds of thousands of DTC brands and Shopify merchants, .store has built a commercial ecosystem that reinforces its own legitimacy.

The honest limitation is trust asymmetry. In direct comparison testing, consumers still exhibit measurably higher trust in .com domains for first-time purchases — particularly at higher price points. This does not make .store a bad choice; it means brands on .store need to invest proportionally more in trust signals (reviews, social proof, security badges, clear policies) to achieve equivalent conversion rates. For brands that execute well on these elements, the trust gap narrows significantly and the benefit of clear commercial intent outweighs the minor extension-related friction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the .store domain legitimate for an online business?

Yes, .store is a fully legitimate ICANN-approved gTLD operated by Radix Registry, one of the world's largest domain registry operators. It is used by well-known brands including Nike and thousands of DTC businesses. The extension is universally accepted by payment processors, shipping platforms, and eCommerce software. Using .store for an online business is no different technically from using .com — the extension carries commercial intent that is appropriate for retail sites.

### How much does a .store domain cost per year?

A .store domain typically costs $20 to $35 per year for standard registration and renewal in 2026. Some registrars offer first-year promotional pricing as low as $5–$10, but verify renewal rates before committing. Premium category terms (fashion.store, food.store, electronics.store) may carry elevated registration prices of $500–$5,000+ designated by Radix Registry.

### Does the .store domain hurt eCommerce SEO?

No. Google treats .store as a standard gTLD with no geographic restrictions. Your .store site will rank based on content quality, product page optimization, backlinks, Core Web Vitals, and technical SEO — not the extension. Running effective eCommerce SEO on .store requires the same fundamentals as any other domain: unique product descriptions, structured data markup, fast load times, and consistent link building.

### What are the most successful brands using .store domains?

Nike is the highest-profile brand using .store (Nike.store for regional storefronts). Google's merchandise store operations use "store" prominently in their URLs. Thousands of DTC brands and Shopify merchants use .store as their primary commerce domain. While .store is not yet as common as .com among globally recognized brands, its adoption by major companies validates its fitness for eCommerce purposes.

### Should I use .store instead of .com for my online shop?

If your preferred .com name is available at a reasonable price, securing the .com is generally advisable due to its higher consumer recognition. .store makes the most sense when your preferred .com is taken or prohibitively expensive, when a descriptive .store URL is more intuitive than your only available .com option, or when you are launching a dedicated storefront separate from an existing .com brand presence.

### Is .store better than .shop for an eCommerce site?

Both .store and .shop are purpose-built eCommerce extensions at comparable price points. .store has been available longer and has stronger recognition in English-language Western markets. .shop has a stronger foothold in Asian markets. If availability and memorability are equal, .store's longer track record and broader Western market recognition give it a slight edge for businesses targeting North American or European audiences.
