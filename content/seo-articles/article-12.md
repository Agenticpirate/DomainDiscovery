This **squarespace domains vs namecheap** comparison will help you understand the real differences — including hidden costs, portability risks, and what happens to your domain if you ever want to move your website.

- Squarespace .com domain renewals average $20–22/yr — approximately 57% more expensive than Namecheap's $13.98/yr renewal

## The Core Question: Convenience vs Control

The central tension in this debate is simple:

- **Squarespace Domains** prioritizes convenience — one dashboard, one billing date, zero configuration.
- **Namecheap** prioritizes control — lower prices, advanced settings, and full independence from any website builder.

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on your priorities, technical comfort level, and long-term plans.

## Squarespace Domain Cost: What You Actually Pay

### Registration Pricing

Squarespace includes a free domain for the first year with annual website plans (Personal, Business, Commerce). After the first year, domain renewals are charged separately. Here's where it gets important:

A .com domain through Squarespace typically renews at around **$20–$22/year** — noticeably higher than registrar-only pricing. You're paying a platform premium.

### What's Included

- Free WHOIS privacy
- Free SSL certificate (via the Squarespace hosting plan, not the domain itself)
- Auto-renewal management
- Built-in DNS configured for Squarespace hosting

### What's Not Obvious

The "free domain" that comes with Squarespace plans is only free for year one. If you cancel your Squarespace plan, you keep the domain — but you'll pay Squarespace's renewal price going forward, which is above market rate.

## Domain Portability: The Argument for Registering Separately

This is the most important consideration many website owners overlook.

### What Happens If You Leave Squarespace?

If your domain is registered through Squarespace and you decide to migrate to WordPress, Webflow, Wix, or any other platform, you'll need to transfer your domain out. Domain transfers have a 60-day lock period after registration or certain account changes (ICANN policy). During a migration, this can be a frustrating delay.

More importantly, during the transfer process, you need to:
1. Unlock the domain in Squarespace
2. Get an authorization (EPP) code
3. Initiate the transfer at your new registrar
4. Wait up to 7 days for completion

With a separate registrar like Namecheap, your domain is already independent. Pointing it to a new host is as simple as updating nameservers — no transfer needed, no delay, no risk of downtime.

### Domain as a Business Asset

Think of your domain name the way you think of your phone number or your business address. You wouldn't want your phone provider to also be your landlord. Keeping your domain registration separate from your hosting or website builder gives you cleaner separation of concerns and more leverage with each vendor.

## DNS Configuration

### With Squarespace Domains

When you register through Squarespace, DNS is automatically configured to point to Squarespace's servers. This is great for simplicity — no setup required. But if you want to run third-party tools (a separate email provider, a subdomain for a different app, a CDN), the DNS interface inside Squarespace is basic.

### With Namecheap

Namecheap's Advanced DNS panel gives you full control over all record types: A, CNAME, MX, TXT, SRV, AAAA, and more. You can point your domain to Squarespace hosting from Namecheap — you just update the nameservers or A records. You get the website builder you want and a registrar that doesn't limit your technical options.

## When Should You Register Through Squarespace?

There are legitimate reasons to register your domain directly through Squarespace:

1. **You're brand new to websites** and want zero friction to get online.
2. **You're confident you'll stay on Squarespace long-term** and the premium pricing doesn't bother you.
3. **You got the first year free** and plan to evaluate transferring before the renewal kicks in.

## When Should You Register Through Namecheap?

Register separately with Namecheap if:

1. **You care about long-term costs** — the savings compound over years.
2. **You want platform flexibility** — you might switch from Squarespace to another builder later.
3. **You manage multiple domains** for different projects or clients.
4. **You need advanced DNS control** for email hosting, subdomains, or integrations.

## The Hybrid Approach

The best setup for most small businesses and creators is:

1. Register your domain at Namecheap (or another dedicated registrar).
2. Build your website on Squarespace.
3. Connect the two by updating your domain's nameservers in Namecheap to point to Squarespace.

This gives you the design flexibility of Squarespace with the pricing and portability of a dedicated registrar. It takes about 15 minutes to configure and saves you real money every year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Squarespace charge for a domain renewal in 2026?
Squarespace charges approximately $20–22/year to renew a .com domain in 2026. This is significantly above the market rate, which runs $9–14/year at dedicated registrars like Namecheap, Porkbun, and Cloudflare. The "free" first-year domain included with Squarespace plans does not apply to renewals.

### Can I use Namecheap with Squarespace?
Yes. You can register your domain at Namecheap and connect it to your Squarespace website by updating the nameservers in your Namecheap account to point to Squarespace's DNS servers. This is a straightforward process that takes about 15 minutes and up to 48 hours for DNS propagation.

### What happens to my Squarespace domain if I cancel my Squarespace plan?
If you cancel your Squarespace website plan, you retain ownership of any domain registered through Squarespace. However, you will need to continue paying Squarespace's renewal price for the domain, or initiate a transfer to a cheaper registrar. The domain will not automatically transfer to a new host.

### Is it worth transferring my domain from Squarespace to Namecheap?
Yes, for most users. Transferring a .com domain from Squarespace to Namecheap costs approximately $13.98 (one year's renewal at Namecheap), and saves you roughly $6–8 per year thereafter. The transfer process takes 5–7 days and requires advance planning due to ICANN's 60-day transfer lock policy.

### Does Namecheap provide free WHOIS privacy like Squarespace?
Yes, Namecheap provides free WHOIS privacy through WhoisGuard on most popular TLDs including .com, .net, and .org. Both Squarespace and Namecheap offer this feature at no charge — it is not a differentiator between the two registrars.

### How do I transfer a domain from Squarespace to Namecheap?
To transfer from Squarespace to Namecheap: (1) unlock the domain in your Squarespace Domains dashboard, (2) request an authorization (EPP/transfer) code from Squarespace, (3) start a domain transfer at Namecheap using that code, (4) approve the transfer confirmation email, and (5) wait 5–7 days for completion. Your website remains live during the transfer.
