# Made-Up Words as Domain Names: The Brandable Domain Strategy

> **Quick Answer:** Made-up word domain names — invented words with no prior dictionary meaning — are one of the most effective branding strategies available. They're almost always available to register, offer strong trademark protection, and give your brand total freedom to define its own identity. Notable examples include Google, Etsy, Zapier, and Skype.

Some of the world's most powerful brands share an unusual trait: their names mean absolutely nothing — at least in any traditional dictionary. Google, Skype, Zapier, Twilio, Etsy. None of these words existed before someone invented them. Yet today they're worth billions. The secret behind their success is a concept called the **made-up word domain name**, and it's one of the most underrated strategies in branding and domain selection.

This guide breaks down why invented word domains work, how to create your own, and how to check availability before you fall in love with a name that's already taken.

---

**Key Facts for 2026:**
- Over 360 million domain names are currently registered globally, meaning nearly every common English word .com is taken.
- Invented-word brands like Google, Skype, and Etsy collectively represent over $2 trillion in market capitalization.
- Coined words are among the easiest categories to trademark — the USPTO grants them the highest level of inherent distinctiveness.
- Short invented-word .com domains (5–8 characters) consistently sell for 3–10x more than descriptive keyword domains of similar length in secondary markets.

## Why Made-Up Word Domain Names Work So Well

### They're Almost Always Available

This is the most practical reason founders and marketers turn to invented word domains. When you try to register a common English word or phrase, you'll likely find it taken, premium-priced, or buried under a confusing pile of hyphens and numbers. Made-up words sidestep this entirely. Because no one has thought of your specific invented word before, the .com is often wide open.

### They're Legally Protectable

Generic words like "Insurance" or "Travel" are nearly impossible to trademark. But invented words — especially ones with no prior meaning — are among the strongest candidates for trademark protection. Brands like Kodak and Xerox are textbook examples. Their names were pure inventions, and that uniqueness gave them airtight legal protection.

### They Become the Category

When a brand name is invented, it has no prior associations. That means the brand gets to define what the word means entirely. "Google" now means to search for something online. "Zoom" now means a video call. Your invented word can own a mental space in your audience's mind in a way that descriptive names never can.

## Famous Examples of Invented Word Domain Names

### Google
Derived from "googol" (the number 1 followed by 100 zeros), the founders intentionally misspelled it. The result was a unique, memorable, and highly brandable invented word that now defines how the world searches for information.

### Etsy
According to founder Rob Kalin, "Etsy" came from watching Italian films and wanting a nonsense word that was easy to pronounce and spell. It had no meaning — and now it means handmade goods to millions of people globally.

### Zapier
Combining "zap" (a common tech verb) with a suffix to make it unique, Zapier is a perfect example of a **semi-invented word**. It starts with something familiar and adds just enough originality to feel fresh while still being intuitive.

### Skype
Originally derived from "Sky Peer-to-Peer," the name was shortened and altered until it became "Skype" — a word that didn't exist, rolled off the tongue, and became synonymous with video calling.

## The Brandable Domain Strategy: How to Create Your Own

### Step 1: Define the Feeling, Not the Function

Before you start generating names, get clear on the emotional tone you want your brand to project. Is it playful? Authoritative? Futuristic? Approachable? A made-up word should feel like your brand, even if it doesn't describe it literally.

### Step 2: Use Word-Building Techniques

Several reliable methods help you construct invented words that feel natural and memorable:

**Portmanteau:** Merge two words into one. Instagram = Instant + Telegram. Pinterest = Pin + Interest.

**Add a suffix or prefix:** Take a root word and modify it. Add "-ify," "-ly," "-io," "-er," or "-ify" to create something new. Spotify. Shopify. Calendly.

**Clip or truncate:** Shorten a longer phrase into a tight, punchy word. LinkedIn came from linking professional networks.

**Phonetic play:** Use sounds that feel energetic or trustworthy. Hard consonants like K, X, and Z feel techy and modern. Soft vowel sounds feel friendly and approachable.

**Repeat or rhyme:** Words with internal rhythm are easier to remember. Think of Yoyo, Vimeo, or Canva.

### Step 3: Test for Pronounceability

Your invented word domain name should be something a person can say aloud after seeing it once, and spell correctly after hearing it once. If your target audience would need to ask "how do you spell that?" every time, it's too complex.

### Step 4: Check Global Connotations

A name that sounds great in English might mean something unfortunate in another language. Before committing to your invented word, run it through a quick check in Spanish, French, German, and any other languages relevant to your market.

### Step 5: Check Domain Availability Immediately

Ideas move fast. As soon as you land on a candidate name, check if the domain is available before you invest emotional energy in it. There's nothing more frustrating than building an entire brand concept around a name only to discover it's been registered for a decade.

## Common Mistakes When Choosing Made-Up Word Domains

### Making It Too Abstract
There's a balance between invented and incomprehensible. A word like "Xlqrtz" is technically made up, but no one can pronounce it, spell it, or remember it. The best invented word domains feel like they could have been real words — they just weren't yet.

### Ignoring the .com
While alternative TLDs have their place, the .com extension still carries the most trust and memorability. If your invented word is only available as a .net or .biz, it may be worth going back to the drawing board.

### Forgetting to Trademark Search
Domain availability and trademark availability are separate things. You can register a domain that uses a trademarked name — and then face legal action later. Always run your invented word through a trademark database before committing.

## H3: How Long Should an Invented Word Domain Be?

Research consistently shows that shorter domain names perform better in terms of memorability and type-in traffic. Aim for:

- **5–8 characters** as the sweet spot
- **No more than 3 syllables** for easy verbal sharing
- **No hyphens or numbers**, which add friction and confusion

Some iconic invented word brands: Google (6 letters), Etsy (4 letters), Skype (5 letters), Zapier (6 letters). Short, crisp, and completely ownable.

Once you've settled on your made-up word domain name and confirmed it's available, the branding work begins. Lean into the fact that it's a blank slate. Your marketing, your product experience, and your community will define what the word means. That's an enormous creative advantage.

- Messaging that explains what you do (since the name won't)
- Social handles that match your domain across every platform

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a made-up word domain name effective?
An effective invented word domain is short (5–8 characters), easy to pronounce and spell after one exposure, free from hyphens and numbers, and meaningless enough in existing languages to be fully trademark-able. The best examples — Google, Etsy, Zapier — feel like they could have been real words, which makes them sticky without being confusable.

### Are made-up word domains still available in 2026?
Yes. While the most obvious invented words have been registered, the combination space of 5–8 letter invented words is enormous. Systematic use of word-building techniques — portmanteau, suffix play, phonetic construction — still yields available .com domains regularly. Checking availability immediately after brainstorming is essential since good names get registered quickly.

### Do invented word domains hurt SEO?
In the short term, invented word domains carry no keyword authority in search results. However, this is not a material disadvantage. Google ranks content, not domain names, for most searches. The SEO benefits of brand recognition — higher click-through rates, direct traffic, brand searches — far outweigh any theoretical advantage from keyword-matching domains over the long term.

### How do I trademark an invented word domain?
File a trademark application with the relevant national office (USPTO in the United States) for your invented word in your business's relevant classes of goods and services. Wholly invented words with no prior meaning — called "fanciful marks" — receive the strongest trademark protection available and are rarely refused registration on distinctiveness grounds. Always perform a trademark clearance search before filing.

### What's the difference between an invented word and a portmanteau?
An invented word has no identifiable component words — it is created entirely from phonetic construction (e.g., Kodak, Xerox). A portmanteau blends two recognizable words (e.g., Instagram = instant + telegram, Pinterest = pin + interest). Both strategies produce highly brandable domain names; portmanteaus carry slightly more inherent meaning while invented words offer maximum originality.

### Can I build a serious business on a name nobody recognizes?
Absolutely. Every dominant brand name started as an unknown word. Recognition is built through marketing, product quality, and customer experience — not by choosing a pre-recognized word. In fact, a name with no prior associations is easier to brand because you define its meaning entirely, without fighting the baggage attached to common words.

### How long should I spend brainstorming invented word domains?
A focused two-to-three hour session using structured techniques (portmanteau, suffix play, truncation, phonetic construction) can generate 50–100 candidates. From that pool, expect 5–15 to be available as .com domains. Running candidates through DomainsDiscovery.com after each brainstorming sprint keeps the process efficient and prevents emotional attachment to unavailable names.
