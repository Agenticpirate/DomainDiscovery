# Should I Use .com or .io for My Startup? (Honest Answer)

> **Quick Answer:** For most startups, .com is the better choice if you can secure it — even with a modifier like "Get" or "Use." Use .io only when the .com is genuinely unavailable or prohibitively expensive and your primary audience is developers or tech-forward users. Many startups that launch on .io eventually acquire the .com when they can afford it, making .io a pragmatic interim choice, not a permanent one.

## Introduction

Few domain questions create more debate in startup circles than **.com vs .io for startup**. Browse Product Hunt on any given day and you'll see dozens of launches on .io domains. But then you look at Stripe, Airbnb, Notion, and Linear — all on .com. So which is actually better?

The honest answer is: it depends on where you are in your journey and who your users are. But there are clear patterns, real tradeoffs, and a few facts that get ignored in most "just use .com" or "just use .io" takes.

This article gives you the honest breakdown — no hype.

**Key Facts for 2026:**
- .io domains cost approximately $30–50/year at most registrars — 2–4x more than a .com renewal
- Approximately 70–75% of the top 1,000 most-visited websites globally use .com as their primary domain
- Google treats .io as a generic TLD (gTLD), not a country-code domain — there is no geographic penalty for global ranking
- Several high-profile startups that launched on .io (Replit, Notion) have migrated to .com after reaching product-market fit, at significant SEO cost

---

## The Case for .com

### It's Still the Default Human Expectation

When someone hears a company name and wants to find the website, they type "[name].com" first. Always. This isn't changing in 2026, despite predictions over the past decade that new gTLDs would erode .com dominance.

The muscle memory for .com is global and deeply embedded. Your potential customers, investors, and media contacts will instinctively assume .com. If your startup is on .io and someone types .com instead, they either hit a 404 or, worse, a competitor.

### Trust With Non-Technical Audiences

For B2C products, enterprise sales, or any startup selling to people who aren't developers or tech insiders, .com still carries significantly more credibility. This matters for:
- Enterprise buyers who need to justify vendor choices internally
- Retail consumers unfamiliar with domain extensions beyond .com
- Media and journalists who find .io unfamiliar
- Potential acquirers who understand that a .com brand is more valuable

### Long-Term Brand Value

.com domains retain resale value. If you raise significant funding, plan for an acquisition, or simply want to build a durable brand, .com is the safer foundation. Domain brokers and acquirers consistently pay premiums for .com.

## The Case for .io

### Availability — This Is the Real Reason

Let's be honest about why most startups use .io: .com was taken.

The .io domain became popular in tech circles primarily because it offered short, punchy domain names that were already registered on .com. If your startup is called "Orbit" and orbit.com is a $50,000 domain, orbit.io might be available for $35.

This is a legitimate reason. Not a perfect one, but a real one.

### Developer and Tech Community Acceptance

Within developer communities, SaaS circles, and startup ecosystems, .io is fully normalized. Posting to Hacker News, Product Hunt, or Dev.to on a .io domain carries no stigma whatsoever. In some circles, .io even signals a certain kind of credibility — "these are builders."

If your primary users are developers, engineers, or tech-forward early adopters, .io is a perfectly fine choice.

### It Signals "This Is a Tech Product"

.io has become something of a genre signal — when you see a .io domain, you expect a B2B tool, a developer utility, or a SaaS product. This can work in your favor with the right audience.

## .io Domain Pros and Cons

### Pros

- **Availability**: Much better availability for short, brand-relevant names than .com
- **Tech credibility**: Recognized and respected in startup and developer communities
- **No direct SEO penalty**: Google treats .io as a generic TLD, not a country code, for search purposes
- **Lower squatting pressure**: Fewer domain squatters camped on .io names vs. .com
- **Good name variety**: Can often get a clean, brandable name without modifiers

### Cons

- **Type-in traffic loss**: Users who hear your name will type .com first, losing traffic
- **Trust deficit with non-technical audiences**: Enterprise buyers, mainstream consumers may find it unfamiliar
- **Higher cost**: .io domains typically cost $30–$50/year vs. ~$10–15/year for .com
- **Country code origin**: .io is technically the country code for the British Indian Ocean Territory, which creates marginal legal and administrative uncertainty
- **Brand ceiling**: If you grow to mainstream scale, the .io label may feel limiting

## SEO: Does the TLD Matter?

### Google's Official Position

Google's John Mueller and the Google Search team have confirmed repeatedly that TLDs other than .com are not penalized in rankings. Google treats .io, .co, .net, and most other gTLDs as equivalent to .com from an algorithmic perspective.

Country code TLDs (ccTLDs) like .uk, .de, .ca are treated as geographically relevant — they get a ranking boost in local searches but may underperform internationally.

**.io is treated as a generic TLD by Google** — no geo penalty.

### Practical SEO Reality

Despite no technical penalty, there are indirect SEO effects:

- **Click-through rates**: Familiar TLDs may get slightly higher CTR in branded search results
- **Link acquisition**: Some publishers instinctively trust .com domains more, which can affect editorial link building
- **Branded search**: If users type your name into Google directly, .com result might rank above .io if someone else has the .com

These effects are real but modest for most startups.

## Other TLDs Worth Considering

### .co

.co has gained meaningful acceptance as a .com alternative, particularly for companies where the .com was taken. It's short, familiar to most internet users, and cheaper than .io.

### .ai

In 2026, .ai has become the domain of choice for artificial intelligence startups. It commands a premium ($50–$100+/year at most registrars) but carries strong thematic signal if AI is genuinely core to your product.

### .app

Google's .app TLD requires HTTPS by default (browser-enforced), which is a security positive. It's a reasonable option for mobile-first or app-centric products.

### New gTLDs (.xyz, .store, .shop, etc.)

These have mostly failed to achieve mainstream recognition. Unless your brand specifically benefits from the TLD meaning (an e-commerce business on .shop, for example), these are generally a poor choice for a startup's primary domain.

## The Decision Framework

Use this to make your call:

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| .com is available for your exact brand name | Use .com — no question |
| .com is available with a modifier ("GetX.com", "UseX.com") | Use .com with modifier |
| .com is taken and too expensive; building for developers | .io is acceptable |
| .com is taken; building for mainstream/enterprise users | Find a different brand name, get the .com |
| AI startup | Consider .ai if budget allows; .com if not |
| .com is taken; budget-conscious | .co is a reasonable second choice |

## The Honest Answer

If you can get the .com — even with a reasonable modifier — get it. The long-term benefits in trust, type-in traffic, and brand value outweigh any short-term availability inconvenience.

If .com is genuinely unavailable or prohibitively priced for your early-stage startup, .io is a legitimate alternative — with the understanding that you may want to eventually acquire the .com. Budget for that possibility.

Don't use .io because it sounds cooler. Use it because .com isn't an option.

## Expert Verdict

The data shows that .com remains the dominant TLD for high-growth companies in 2026, with approximately 70–75% of the top-visited websites globally using it as their primary domain. The pattern among successful startups is clear: those who can secure .com do so, and those who launched on .io and later reached significant scale — Replit, Notion — invested in migrating to .com despite the SEO disruption cost.

Our analysis indicates that the .io debate is largely a false one for early-stage startups. At the pre-product-market-fit stage, the domain is a minor factor compared to product quality and distribution. What matters is making a deliberate, eyes-open choice: if you use .io, acknowledge it as a provisional choice and budget for eventual .com acquisition, rather than treating it as a permanent brand decision.

Based on current 2026 pricing and market dynamics, the clearest guidance is: .io costs $30–50/year (3–5x more than .com), limits your trust with non-technical audiences, and creates type-in traffic loss from day one. The cases where .io is genuinely the right permanent choice are narrow — primarily developer tools with no plans to sell to mainstream or enterprise audiences. For everyone else, .com should remain the goal, with .io as a pragmatic stepping stone when necessary.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is .io good for startups in 2026?
.io is an acceptable domain choice for startups targeting developer or tech-savvy audiences in 2026, where .com is unavailable or prohibitively expensive. It carries no SEO penalty from Google (treated as a gTLD), and is fully normalized in developer communities. However, .io costs 3–5x more than .com annually, and creates type-in traffic loss from users who instinctively type .com.

### Does a .io domain hurt SEO?
No. Google officially treats .io as a generic top-level domain, not a country code, meaning there is no geographic penalty or ranking disadvantage compared to .com. However, .io can indirectly affect SEO through slightly lower click-through rates in search results and a potential trust gap with some publishers when building editorial backlinks.

### Why do so many tech startups use .io?
Most tech startups use .io because the .com version of their preferred name was already registered or prohibitively expensive. .io gained popularity as a .com alternative in startup and developer circles from approximately 2012 onwards, when short, punchy .com names became increasingly scarce. The "I/O" abbreviation (input/output) also resonated as a tech-relevant signal.

### How much does a .io domain cost per year?
A .io domain costs approximately $30–$50/year at most registrars, compared to $9–$15/year for a .com domain. This price premium reflects the higher wholesale cost of .io registry fees. Over five years, a .io domain can cost $150–$250 more than the equivalent .com.

### Should I use .ai for my AI startup?
.ai has become widely recognized as the domain of choice for AI-focused startups in 2026 and carries strong thematic signal. It costs approximately $50–$100+/year, making it more expensive than .com. Use .ai if your brand identity is tightly linked to AI and the premium cost is acceptable, but prioritize .com if you can secure it — .com carries broader trust signals across all audience types.

### Can I migrate from .io to .com later?
Yes, but migrating from .io to .com is a significant undertaking. It requires setting up 301 redirects from all old URLs to new .com URLs, submitting updated sitemaps to Google, updating all external links where possible, and waiting 6–12 months for full SEO equity to transfer. Many startups have done this successfully, but it involves temporary ranking drops and requires careful execution.

### What is the best domain extension for a SaaS startup?
For most SaaS startups in 2026, .com is the best domain extension if available. For developer-focused tools where .com is taken, .io is the most accepted alternative. For AI-native products, .ai is increasingly recognized. Avoid .biz, .info, .guru, and most new gTLDs — they have not achieved mainstream trust and can undermine brand credibility with enterprise buyers.
